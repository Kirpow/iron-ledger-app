Barcode lookup (optional)

The barcode scanner uses Open Food Facts by default — free, no signup, and this works for anyone using the app with no setup. You can also connect a free USDA FoodData Central key, which is often more accurate for U.S. packaged foods and gets offered as a second option whenever a scan looks wrong. This key is only saved on this device — everyone using the app can (and can skip) set up their own.
  1. Open fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-key-signup (a U.S. government site — free, no payment, no approval wait).
  2. Enter your name and email, then submit the form.
  3. Check that email — the key arrives within a couple of minutes.
  4. Copy it and paste it into the field above. That's it.
Not comfortable doing that? Just leave the field blank — barcode scanning keeps working exactly as it does today, no change needed.

Recipe search (optional)

Recipes tab search that finds nothing locally checks TheMealDB by default — free, no signup, but no calorie/macro info. Connect a free Spoonacular key instead and online results come with real macros and log in one tap, same as the built-in recipes. This key is only saved on this device. If it's ever invalid or over its daily limit, search quietly falls back to TheMealDB instead of failing.
  1. Open spoonacular.com/food-api and create a free account — no credit card needed.
  2. Your API key is shown on your account/profile page once you're signed in.
  3. Copy it and paste it into the field above. That's it.
  4. Free plan is 50 points/day (about 45 recipe-with-nutrition lookups) — plenty for personal use.
Not comfortable doing that? Just leave the field blank — online recipe search keeps working via TheMealDB, just without macros.
